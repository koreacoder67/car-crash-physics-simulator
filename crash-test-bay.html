Physics model

  • Each vehicle is a rigid body that moves in x, y and yaw, plus pitch and roll on four suspension corners. Wheel loads shift under braking and impact, and wheels lift off when a spring fully unloads.
  • The body outline is 80 crush nodes. Each one is elastic-plastic: force per metre of width is A + B·c (CRASH3 stiffness), rising steeply once the crush reaches the passenger cell. Door beams resist being pushed in at a single point.
  • Each structure has a height band: bumper rails, sills, or a truck's rear frame. When bands miss each other, the load path is weak and the taller vehicle overrides. This is how underride happens.
  • Contact forces act at the bumper height, so they pitch the body. Restitution is not set; it comes out of the elastic rebound.
  • Up to five dummies per car, or every seat in a bus or shuttle. Rear occupants load the seat back in front of them. Bus passengers have no belts.
  • The curtain airbag unrolls from the roof rail in about 20 ms, inflates its stitched chambers, and dents where a head presses into it. It fires on the struck side.
  • Each driver is a two-mass dummy (torso 70 kg and head 4.5 kg on a neck spring). Restraints are a seatbelt with pretensioner and 16 kN load limit, a front airbag, and side and curtain airbags. Door and dash intrusion reduce the space around the dummy.
  • Fuel system: a crushed engine bay ruptures fuel lines, and crush that reaches the tank ruptures it. Leaked fuel spreads as a 2 mm pool; burning petrol releases about 2.2 MW per m² of pool.
  • Fuel needs an ignition source: an electrical short in the engine bay, sparks from scraping metal, or fuel on a hot exhaust (diesel lights more easily on hot surfaces). Fires grow on t² curves: about 0.01 kW/s² in the engine bay, and 0.047 kW/s² in the cabin once flames get through the firewall.
  • Battery packs are a grid of modules, each with its own temperature. Crushed modules heat up from internal shorts. Above 170 °C a module goes into thermal runaway, releasing 1.5 times its stored energy in about a minute and heating its neighbours, so the fire spreads module by module.
  • Cabin air temperature follows a heat balance and becomes untenable above 120 °C. Radiant heat (30% of the fire’s output, falling off with distance squared) can ignite the other vehicle above about 12 kW/m².
  • Rollover tests treat the car as a rigid body in the side view: tyres, sills, waist rails and roof rails touch the floor and the kerb. Roof rails are elastic-plastic and yield at about 0.8 × the roof strength-to-weight ratio times the vehicle weight, so a weak roof crushes toward the heads. The dummies also move vertically: lap belt, seat cushion, a neck spring, and head-to-roof contact that gives the neck compression (limit about 4 kN).
  • Tyres use a Pacejka-style grip curve with load-dependent grip (μ = 0.8). Step size is 0.1 ms in contact and 1 ms otherwise.
